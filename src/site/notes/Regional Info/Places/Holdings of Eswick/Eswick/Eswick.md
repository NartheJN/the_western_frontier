## Geography
#### Region and Environment
The frontier is a northern European-styled region with a wooded and temperate environment. Summers are warm, while winters are cold. Overcast skies and rainfall are common throughout the region.

#### Natural Features
The town is coastal, though it does not sit directly on the coast. A wall surrounds the town, with three gates facing east, west, and north. Two ports lie north of the town within view of the city, with the older port seeing less use than the newer one. A river runs along the western wall, following a slight meander. Beyond the farmland surrounding the town, forests stretch to the east and west.

A tree brought over from the Empire stands at the centre of the town’s park. It was planted as a symbol of Eswick’s connection to the Empire and serves as a reminder of the settlement’s origins. The tree has since become a familiar part of the town, standing at the heart of a space that is open to everyone.

Once every three lunar cycles, though never on a predictable schedule, the moon exerts an unusual influence on the waters around Eswick. The tides shift in strange ways, sometimes exposing parts of the riverbed that are normally submerged before suddenly returning, while the water itself may take on an unusual glow. The changing currents alter where fish gather, making fishing during the event unpredictable and forcing local fishermen to adapt their usual practices. For other residents, the shifting tides can temporarily expose unfamiliar stretches of the riverbed, alter paths along the water, and make familiar areas difficult or dangerous to navigate when the tide suddenly returns.    

### Weather
The town has warm summers and cold winters. Overcast skies and rainfall are common throughout the year.

## Society
#### Customs
Wind chimes can be found hanging outside every house in town, a common measure used to deal with the local bats. Nearly every house is also built with patios arranged in particular ways, intended to help ward off various dangers.

The town takes great pride in the quality of its fish, with fishing being an important part of local life. Fishing competitions are held as a way for the townspeople to celebrate this. 

The town observes a number of annual celebrations: 
- Firbolg Peace Alliance Day 
- Landing Day.
    
#### Religion
Religion plays an influential role in daily life within the town, though there is growing tension within the church itself. Some of its members are known for being zealous and overbearing, while others take a less forceful approach. This division has also contributed to issues between the church and the town’s rangers.

The town also experiences strange occurrences involving spirits, which some believe may be related to differences in religious beliefs. 

Aldren Vey, the local cleric of [[Regional Info/Pantheon/The Prime Deities/Ramor\|Ramor]], is a well-known figure within the community that is currently keeping the peace between both sides and mediating disuptes.

## Government
#### City Officers
The town’s affairs are overseen by a five-member council, whose seats are divided among several of the community’s most influential figures. Edwin Merrin, a halfling who owns and operates the local pawnshop, serves as its leader, chosen by the people of the town after previous upset. Garrick Voss, the wealthy-born Dockmaster whose position gives him a strong interest in the town’s ports and whose habit of keeping track of everyone’s business makes him a well-informed member. Valerian Thorne, one of the three Arcanists who arrived with the original expedition, holds the remaining politically influential seat. Ambitious and backed by a wealthy family and connections within the Empire, Thorne brings a strong connection to the wider Imperial establishment.

The council is rounded out by two representatives with closer ties to the everyday concerns of the town. Marta Venn, a local farmer and landholder, speaks for the farmers and rural residents surrounding the settlement and is chiefly concerned with farmland, food supplies, and the safety of those living beyond the walls; and Oren Calder, an older dwarf and established blacksmith, gives the town’s craftsmen and working population a voice. Gruff and practical, Calder has little patience for political disputes and prefers to focus on immediate, tangible concerns. 
Together, the five members represent a mixture of local, commercial, rural, and Imperial interests within the town’s government. 

#### Crime
Crime in the town is most prevalent around the port, particularly within the older docks where illegal activities are known to take place. The area also contains the remains of a block of buildings destroyed when the estuary flooded, leaving behind ruins that have become associated with danger and criminal activity.
The north-western outskirts of the town are generally avoided as well, due to the presence of a sinkhole and the danger of more.

#### Foreign Relations
The town was established as a frontier settlement for the Empire, serving its efforts to explore, colonise, and expand its reach into the surrounding region. Despite this purpose, relations with the Empire are not universally positive, and there is a growing undercurrent of anti-Empire sentiment among the townspeople. Rumours that the Empire intends to take over the town have only added to these tensions, as have whispers that an imperial spy may be operating within the community. The Empire's Envoy has done little to quiet these concerns, appearing to withhold information from the town.

Relations with the local Firbolg tribes remain complicated. The first contact between the town and the Firbolgs was violent, leaving tensions that have lingered even after peace was brokered. Firbolg Peace Alliance Day is held each year to mark that agreement. The town's rangers, led by a Firbolg from a local tribe, sit at the centre of some of these tensions. Their close ties to the local Firbolgs have created friction with both the church and some townspeople, however many within the town still respect the Rangers for keeping the roads between towns safe and for the work they do further beyond that, even if a lot of it they aren't told about.

## Trade
Fishing is an important part of the town’s trade, and the town is well known for the quality of its fish. The portside market is built on boats and barges, providing a place where goods can be bought and sold along the water. The port is also regularly visited by privateer ships from distant lands, as well as adventurers, bringing exotic goods, foreign curiosities, and treasures from far beyond the town. Scouts and travelling traders regularly bring local items back to town, which can be collected from a local trading post. The man who lives above the establishment, Eamon Reed, is known for collecting birds and trading information.
Edwin Merrin, a halfling shopowner, runs the town’s pawnshop, though his son has since taken over its day-to-day running and is doing a poor job of it.

The town’s trade is also connected to the Empire, though travel between Eswick and the Empire is dangerous for a number of reasons. The Empire’s flagship makes infrequent journeys across the Stormstrike Sea between Eswick and the Empire, though it is currently the only Imperial ship to make the route with any regularity. The Dockmaster, Garrick Voss, oversees the port and holds considerable influence over the town’s trade.

## Defenses
#### Military/Militia
Eswick maintains a small professional militia, relying on volunteers to swell its ranks whenever the town is threatened. Weapons training forms part of the early education of many of the town’s men, while some women also choose to learn. The militia is commanded by Captain Dorian Hale, a strict and uncompromising officer who places a heavy emphasis on discipline and preparedness. He is blunt to a fault, with little patience for excuses, ceremony, or anyone who wastes his time. Though his manner has made him unpopular with many in town, few question his dedication to keeping Eswick safe.

The scouts serve a different purpose, spending most of their time watching the surrounding countryside and dealing with threats before they can reach the town. They can still be called upon to reinforce Eswick’s defences when needed, but their primary role is preventative. The scouts are led by Rhovan, a Firbolg from a local tribe, and include a grumpy old ranger who is widely respected despite his temperament.

In more serious situations, Eswick can turn to it's Arcanists, though Garran Veyr is considered a last resort. His powerful magic makes him a danger not only to his enemies but to everything around him, with the potential for significant collateral damage. His difficulty working alongside others only adds to the reluctance to call upon him unless the situation truly demands it.

## History
#### Founding
Eswick was established as a frontier settlement of the Empire, founded to explore, colonise, and extend the Empire’s reach into the surrounding region. The original expedition included three Arcanists, whose tower was constructed upon the ruins of an older structure a short distance from the fledgling settlement.

The first settlement, now known as Scar-Town, was destroyed after tensions between the expedition and the local Firbolgs erupted into open conflict. The final act of aggression was the torching of the town, leaving much of the expedition displaced. The Arcanists provided shelter around their tower and over time the new settlement grew up around it. The Arcanist Tower remains one of Eswick’s oldest landmarks and to some the most important.

As the new settlement took root, Eswick reached several important milestones. Peace between the Expedition and the Firbolg Tribes was accepted, The first Imperial ship successfully made the journey to the town, establishing a connection with the Empire; the surrounding farmland produced its first successful harvest, marking an important step in Eswick becoming a stable and established community rather than a fledgling outpost and the Ranger Division was established.

More recently, Eswick was affected by a major outbreak of disease. The town continues to experience more problems with sickness and disease than usual, and the outbreak has left a lasting impression on the community. A sickhouse was later established in an open area of the town, to combat the continued concern surrounding illness among the population.
